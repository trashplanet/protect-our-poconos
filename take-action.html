SPEAK EFFECTIVELY

How to Make Your Comment Count

Strong public comments don't need exaggeration. They need specificity.

1

Be specific

Identify the exact project, ordinance, permit, or application you're addressing.

"I don't want data centers in the Poconos."
"I'm commenting on the proposed Smithfield Gateway conditional-use application and its potential noise impacts on nearby homes."
2

Focus on concrete impacts

Choose the issues that actually matter to you instead of cramming everything into one three-minute comment.

Groundwater & wells Forests & wetlands Grid & electrical demand Noise & generators Traffic & roads Scenic character Jobs & economy Setbacks & buffers Cumulative effects
3

Ask for something

A useful comment tells officials what you want them to do. For example:

  • Require additional groundwater analysis
  • Require stronger setbacks or forest buffers
  • Require enforceable nighttime noise limits
  • Require ongoing noise or groundwater monitoring
  • Shift infrastructure costs to the developer where lawful
  • Deny or delay if standards aren't demonstrated
4

Distinguish fact from concern

It's legitimate to raise an unanswered question. The strongest arguments survive fact-checking.

"The long-term groundwater impact has not yet been established."
"This project will destroy everyone's wells."
5

Make it personal

Officials already have technical reports. What they don't have is your lived experience — and personal doesn't have to mean dramatic.

"My family relies on a private well." "I live within a mile of the proposed site." "I support data centers, but this location needs stronger safeguards."

Build Your Own Message — A Simple Structure

1 · WHO YOU ARE
"I'm a resident of [township]."
2 · WHY
"I'm writing regarding [project or permit]."
3 · YOUR CONCERN
"My primary concern is [specific issue]."
4 · YOUR REQUEST
"I'm asking the township to [specific action]."
5 · CLOSE
"Thank you for considering public input."

Use this structure in your own words. Explain the facts and concerns that matter to you, and follow the receiving agency’s instructions.

SHOWING UP

Attend a Meeting: What to Expect

If you've never attended a township or county meeting before, you're not alone.

BEFORE YOU GO

  • Check the official agenda and location
  • Verify the date immediately before attending
  • Read any project documents you care about
  • Find out whether speakers must sign in
  • Bring written notes; arrive early for hearings

WHEN YOU SPEAK

  • State your name and community if required
  • Stay within the allotted time
  • Lead with your strongest point
  • Address the board, not the audience
  • Ask for a specific action; submit written notes too

FORMAL HEARINGS ARE DIFFERENT

Smithfield's Gateway proceeding is a conditional-use hearing: the applicant must demonstrate compliance with ordinance standards, people may request party status, and decisions can ultimately be appealed to court.
